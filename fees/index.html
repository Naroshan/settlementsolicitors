Will My Employer Pay My Legal Fees?

In the vast majority of settlement agreement cases, yes. Employers almost always make a contribution to your legal costs — and in most cases that contribution covers our fee in full.

📞 020 3058 3365 Request a callback

How Employer Contributions Work

When an employer offers a settlement agreement, they are required to ensure the employee receives independent legal advice before signing. In practice, this means employers almost universally include a contribution towards legal costs within the settlement agreement itself.

This contribution is typically stated as a fixed sum, ranging from £250 plus VAT for simple agreements to £750 or more for complex cases. In many straightforward cases, this contribution covers our fee entirely — meaning there is no cost to you at all.

What We Confirm Upfront

Before beginning any work, we will tell you:

  • What employer contribution is included in your agreement
  • What our fee for reviewing your specific agreement will be
  • Whether there is any shortfall, and how much it is

There are no hidden charges. If the employer contribution does not cover our full fee, we will tell you the exact amount before starting, and you can decide whether to proceed.

Most Employers Cover Legal Fees in Full

The vast majority of our clients pay nothing because their employer’s contribution fully covers our fee. This is particularly common for:

  • Standard redundancy exits
  • Performance-related settlement agreements
  • Negotiated departures at management level and above
  • Agreements where the employer is a medium or large organisation

What if the Contribution Doesn’t Cover Everything?

If your agreement is particularly complex — for example, involving significant financial packages, multiple claims, or detailed restrictive covenants — our fee may exceed the employer contribution. We will always advise you of any shortfall before beginning work and give you a clear, fixed fee quote.

Employment Tribunal and Disputed Claims

For employment tribunal claims and other disputed matters, fees are agreed on a case-by-case basis following an initial assessment. We will give you a clear picture of likely costs before you commit to any course of action. In some cases, conditional fee arrangements may be available.
