Settlement Agreement vs Resignation: Which Protects You?

If things have gone wrong at work and you're thinking about walking away, resigning is not always the safest — or the most valuable — option available to you.

Resignation Gives Up Your Leverage

Once you resign, you have generally chosen to end your own employment, and it is difficult to undo. If you had a valid grievance or legal claim against your employer, resigning without first addressing it can weaken your position — you lose your main point of leverage, which is your continued employment and the risk your employer faces if the relationship ends badly for them.

What a Settlement Agreement Offers Instead

A settlement agreement is a negotiated exit: your employer pays you an agreed sum, and in return you agree not to bring employment claims against them. Unlike resigning, this route lets you extract value from the situation rather than simply walking away, and it can be pursued whether or not you have already resigned — though your negotiating position is generally stronger before you do.

When Resigning Still Makes Sense

There is one important exception: if your employer has fundamentally breached your contract — for example through serious bullying, an unreasonable demotion, or persistent failure to address a legitimate grievance — resigning and bringing a constructive dismissal claim may be the right route, particularly if your employer shows no willingness to engage or negotiate. This is a higher-risk path than a negotiated exit, since you carry the burden of proving the breach, so it should not be decided without advice.

Raising the Possibility of a Settlement Yourself

You do not have to wait for your employer to offer a settlement agreement. Employees can raise the idea of a negotiated exit directly, often through what is known as a protected conversation, which allows a frank discussion about ending the employment relationship without it being used against you later if no agreement is reached. Approaching this the wrong way can weaken your position, so it is worth taking advice on how to raise it before you do.
